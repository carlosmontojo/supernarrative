# Chapter 11: A Voice in the Rock

Corr stopped saying a month.

That was how Jonah knew he'd done something that could not be undone. He came back up to the Shallows on the sixth day, walking, with Ulli behind him because Ulli did not believe he would make the road, and he made the road, and he walked down it past four decuries eating on the rock, and every man on it stopped and looked at him the way the transport hold had looked at him after Hask, and Corr, sitting against the wall with his bowl, looked up and opened his mouth and closed it and did not say a month.

He didn't say anything. He looked at Jonah's face, and then at the wall of the gallery behind Jonah, as if the wall had done something, and then he looked at his bowl and did not look up again.

"It doesn't turn around," Bricks said. He was standing by the bucket with his wrist splinted against his chest in a strip of Decimus's cloth. He said it the way he said cut a shard, without weight, as a thing that was so. "Nineteen years I've heard of. Auger's heard of nineteen. It doesn't."

"It didn't," Jonah said.

"Then what did?"

He didn't have an answer that Bricks could carry, so he didn't give one. He went to his place at the wall of the seventh chamber and picked up a chisel that somebody had left, and put it to the dark, and the dark was warm, and it opened for him like a door, and he cut one shard, slowly, and dropped it in the bucket, and his arms did not shake at all, and he made them.

---

Tullus had a rule for the ones who came back, and the rule was that they had not been sick, they had been absent, and absence was six days of quota that decury six had carried for him, and a man who was carried paid.

He paid at night. Decimus told him at the dusk count, reading it off the list without looking up, in the voice he used for things that were not personal. Four four seven one, punishment detail, thirty nights, the haul. And then, still not looking up, in a voice that was: "You'll want to eat first. It's a long way down."

The haul was buckets. The Deep cut more than the Shallows, because the Vein was thicker there and came off in slabs, and the slabs went into buckets and the buckets went up to the cage, and the cage came down once a shift, and in between the buckets sat at the Deep's end of the road and somebody carried them. It was the worst detail in the Pit. Everybody said so. It was the Deep in the dark with a bucket in each hand for three hours past the end of the day, on legs that had already stood at a wall for twelve, and the road went past the Cradle.

He'd have chosen it. He'd have paid for it, if there had been anything to pay with. Thirty nights on a road past a wall where something tapped.

The first night he carried and did not stop, because there was a Deep foreman at the buckets and a guard at the cage and men on the road, and he learned the road instead. He learned where the lamps were, too far apart, and where between them the dark was whole. He learned that the Cradle was empty, that nobody had been laid in it since him, and that the men who passed its mouth did not look in. He learned that past it the road went on for two hundred paces and ended at a steel door set flush in the rock, without a handle, without a slot, with a lamp beside it that was not lit, and that the Deep foreman, when Jonah's eyes went to it, said, "Don't," and nothing else.

The second night he stopped.

He stopped in the whole dark between two lamps with a bucket in each hand and set them down and put his knuckles to the rock, on the Cradle side, where the wall was warm, and tapped. Four, four, seven, one. And waited. And the road was silent, and the Vein breathed behind the rock the way it did, kind, wanting him, and nothing tapped back, and after a count of a hundred he picked up the buckets and went on.

The third night the same.

The fourth night, halfway through the count, the rock said: LATE.

---

He would think about that word for years. Not the lesson. The word. He'd stood in the dark with a bucket at each foot and his knuckles on the rock and heard a thing behind a steel door with no lamp tap the one word, in his own code, done badly, and he had laughed out loud on the Deep road with the Vein breathing beside him, because it was the first thing anyone had said to him in a month that was not a number or a warning or a month, and it was a joke.

Late. As if there'd been an appointment. As if he'd kept a thing waiting that had been waiting twenty years.

I had buckets, he tapped.

EVERYONE HAS BUCKETS. YOU HAVE THIRTY NIGHTS. I HAVE COUNTED THREE. DO NOT WASTE THEM.

Who are you?

THE ROCK.

He laughed again. He couldn't help it. Somewhere down the road a lamp swung, the Deep foreman coming back for a count, and he picked up the buckets and went, and the rock said nothing else that night, and he carried the two words down the road with him like something warm.

Late. The rock.

---

The lessons started on the fifth night and he did not know they were lessons until the seventh.

The rock asked questions. That was all, at first. It tapped, slow and wrong and getting less wrong, and it asked things, and he answered because there was nobody else in the Pit who asked him anything.

DOES IT COME OUT AT NIGHT.

He knew what it meant. It had been coming out. Since the Cradle. Since the mouthful. The thing under his ribs had come back through his hands fed and had rested, for the first time, and rest had done something to it that hunger never had. It was stronger. He felt it in the day, leaning, quieter, not pulling at the wall anymore, or not the way it had, but at night on the shelf it came up the way it had come up in his father's room, into his arms and back, ready, and it did not always wait to be asked. Twice it had put plate on his hands in his sleep. Once on his face. He'd woken with the dark glass over his mouth and the taste of it and had lain very still with Pell breathing on the shelf above until it went back in.

Yes, he tapped.

HOW DO YOU HOLD IT.

I don't. I push it down. Like holding breath.

THAT IS WHY IT COMES OUT. There was a pause. YOU CANNOT HOLD BREATH FOR SIX YEARS. WHAT DID YOU DO IN THE CRADLE.

Said names.

DO THAT.

He stood in the dark and did not understand, and then, slowly, did. The rock did not explain. That was the thing about it. It said a thing and stopped and let him find the rest of it on the road, with the buckets, and he found it that night, lying on the shelf with the plate trying to come out on the backs of his hands and Pell asleep above him. He did not push it down. He had pushed it down for a month and it had come out anyway, in his sleep, over his mouth. He lay still instead, and said, under his breath, into the blanket, the way he had in the Cradle:

"Tom Vale."

The plate on his hands stopped.

"Mara Ansel. Ochoa. Pell. He's right there, he's asleep, he sings."

It went in. Not because he'd pushed. Because it had heard the names, and it had stayed for them once, at a door, and something in it had learned that when the names were being said it was where it wanted to be, which was in, listening, and it went in and lay behind his heart and listened, and he said the whole of the list, quietly, to the end, and by the end it was asleep, if it slept, and so was he.

The rock, the next night, asked how many he had said.

Fourteen.

SAY THEM EVERY NIGHT. ADD ONE WHEN YOU HAVE ONE. IT WILL COME TO EXPECT THEM. THINGS THAT EXPECT DO NOT WANDER.

What are you, he tapped, that you know that.

OLD.

---

The settling was the second thing.

He had not known it had a name. He'd called it the fever. It had come on him in the rings and after Varro and after the shackle and, worst, after the Cradle, three days of it, sweating on the shelf with the shard quota still owed, and every time it came it was because the thing under his ribs had eaten, and every time it was as if his body had been given a bigger coat and had to grow into it while it was on.

It came again on the ninth night of the haul. Not from eating. From something else. He'd been carrying buckets past the Cradle with the road empty and had felt it start, the heat from the middle out, and had thought, no, I haven't, it hasn't, and had understood, standing there with the buckets, that it had. Not a mouthful. Not a shard. It had been eating him. The whole time, in the day, at the wall, quietly, the way a man eats bread while he works without noticing the bread. It had been taking something from the standing and the cutting and the carrying, some small thing, the way it had taken the yoke in the rings, and the taking had added up, and it was, he understood, a little bigger than it had been. Not from the Vein. From him. From thirty nights of the haul. It had grown on the work.

He put the buckets down and put his knuckles on the rock and tapped, badly, because his hands were shaking: It's happening again. The fever. I didn't feed it.

YOU DID. YOU CARRIED. IT LEARNS FROM THE BODY WHEN THE BODY IS USED HARD. THAT IS WHAT THE FEVER IS. NOT SICKNESS. GROWTH. IT WILL HAPPEN FOR THE REST OF YOUR LIFE. SIT DOWN.

He sat down. In the dark, on the road, with his back to the warm rock.

BREATHE. COUNT THE BREATHS. IN THE OLD TONGUE.

I don't know the old tongue.

YOU SAID THE PRAYER TO MARS IN IT IN FRONT OF A PROVINCE WITH THE ENDINGS RIGHT. I HEARD.

He sat very still.

He had said it. In the Cradle. He'd said, Livia Vibia, she asked my name, and he'd said Priscus, and he'd said, I'm going to be precise, and somewhere in it he'd said the square and the sand and the prayer, because he'd been saying everything, all of it, to a thing in a doorway, and there had been a rock behind his head, and the rock had been listening.

You heard all of it, he tapped.

EVERY NAME. I AM THE ROCK. IT IS WHAT I DO. Then, after a moment: UNUS. THAT IS ONE. DUO. TRES. QUATTUOR. BREATHE ON EACH. SLOWLY. THE FEVER GOES FASTER IF THE BODY IS BUSY WITH SOMETHING THAT IS NOT THE FEVER.

He breathed. Unus. Duo. Tres. He got to twenty and the rock taught him twenty, and he got to fifty and it taught him fifty, in taps, in the dark, with the Vein breathing beside him and the heat going out of him a little on each number, and by the time the Deep foreman's lamp came down the road he was on his feet with the buckets and the fever was a thing he could carry, and he knew how to count to a hundred in the language of the men who had put him here.

The rock, before the lamp reached him, tapped one more thing.

QUICK. YOU WILL DO.

---

He learned the rock the way he'd learned the hold. By paying attention.

He learned that it was a man. Not the Vein, not a dead thing, not whatever Ulli's old ones said. A man, because it was tired some nights and sharp others, because it made mistakes in the code and corrected them, because on the fourteenth night it did not answer at all and on the fifteenth it tapped, first, before he'd said his number, VISITORS, and nothing else, and he understood that Tullus's men had been down with their lamp.

He learned that it had been listening to Ferrum men through that wall for a long time, because the code got better every night, faster, the gaps closing, until by the twentieth night it was tapping yard-tap the way Bram tapped it, and then better than Bram, with a rhythm to it that Bram had never had.

He learned that it was funny. That was the thing he had not expected and the thing that kept him walking past the Cradle every night with his legs gone to water. It was dry, and it was quick, and it was funny the way Mara was funny, upward, at the thing that deserved it. He'd tapped, on the eleventh night, that the Deep foreman had told him the buckets were lighter if you thought about the Princeps, and the rock had tapped back, without a pause, THE PRINCEPS THINKS ABOUT BUCKETS EVERY DAY. HE HAS A GREAT MANY. And Jonah had stood there in the dark and laughed until he had to put the buckets down.

He tried to get one past it. He couldn't. He'd tapped, the sixteenth night, I saw a Flamen once who couldn't read his own glass, and the rock had said, ONLY ONCE? THEY PROMOTE THOSE, and on the seventeenth he'd said, Corr says a month, and the rock had said, CORR HAS SAID A MONTH TO SIX HUNDRED MEN. HE IS THE MOST OPTIMISTIC MAN IN THE PIT. HE HAS BEEN RIGHT ABOUT NONE OF THEM, and Jonah had had to lean on the wall.

And under the jokes, every night, a lesson, and he had stopped not knowing they were lessons. Say the names. Count the breaths. Do not push it, ask it. When it comes out on your hands in the day, and it will, put your hands in the bucket, the Vein's shards will hide the plate's colour from a lamp for the length of a breath and that is all you need. When it comes out on your face, turn to the wall. Never let it eat at the wall in front of a man, the wall goes dark where it eats and Corr is a fool but he is not blind.

WHAT DO THEY CALL YOU ON THE ROAD, it asked, on the twenty-first night.

Cut one. Four four seven one. Ghost, sometimes. The one who turned around.

AND BEFORE.

Vale.

I KNOW THAT. I MEAN BEFORE THAT. IN THE YARDS.

He thought. It was a long way back.

Jonah, he tapped. Just that. My mother chose it. My father says it's a sailor's word. A man who's bad luck for a ship.

The rock was quiet for a moment.

THEN SHE CHOSE WELL, it said. YOU HAVE BEEN VERY BAD LUCK FOR AT LEAST ONE SHIP. And then, before he could answer, quicker: HOLD OUT YOUR HAND. THE RIGHT. BRING THE PLATE UP ON THE BACK OF IT. ONLY THE BACK. ONLY THE HAND. THEN PUT IT AWAY. DO IT UNTIL THE LAMP COMES.

He did it. He stood in the dark with the buckets at his feet and brought the plate up on the back of his right hand, only there, only that, a patch of dark glass the size of a palm, and put it away, and brought it up, and put it away, and the thing under his ribs, which had never in its life been asked for so little, did it, interested, and did it again, and by the thirtieth time it was doing it before he finished asking, and the fever came, small, warm, the way it came now, and he counted it down in the old tongue and did not stop.

Something clicked. Small. The lock going over.

He knew it now. He'd felt it in the sand and in the transport hold and in the Cradle and he knew what it was, and he stood in the dark of the Deep road with plate on the back of his hand and felt the coat get bigger by a size he could almost name, and thought, that's from nothing. That's from a hand. That's from doing a thing thirty times in the dark because a rock said to.

Good, the rock said, as if it had felt it too. THAT IS DRILL. IT IS SLOW. IT IS THE ONLY ONE OF THE THREE YOU CAN DO WITHOUT ANYONE DYING. LEARN TO LIKE IT.

What are the other two?

The rock did not answer. The lamp was coming down the road.

---

On the twenty-ninth night he asked it the thing he'd been carrying since the fourth.

He'd carried it the way he carried the buckets, in both hands, all the way down and all the way back, and had not put it down because he did not know what would be under it. He put it down now, in the whole dark between two lamps, with his knuckles on the warm rock.

Why me.

The rock said nothing for a long time. Long enough that he thought it had gone, or that the men had been down, or that he'd tapped it wrong.

Then: BECAUSE YOU TURNED AROUND.

That's not an answer.

IT IS THE ONLY ONE THERE IS. There was a pause, and the tapping when it came again was slower, and he had learned enough of the rock by then to hear that the slowness was not tiredness. I HAVE BEEN BEHIND THIS DOOR TWENTY YEARS. I HAVE HEARD EVERY MAN THEY LAID IN THAT CRADLE GO INTO THE WALL. I HAVE TAPPED TO SOME. NONE HEARD. THE WALL HAD THEM BEFORE THEY COULD LISTEN. AND THEN A CUT ONE LAY DOWN IN IT AND STARTED SAYING NAMES, AND HE SAID MINE.

Jonah stood very still.

I didn't say your name. I don't know it.

YOU SAID VIBIUS.

The Vein breathed. The road was empty. Somewhere far up the shaft a bell rang for the change of shift and he did not hear it.

I don't understand, he tapped.

NO. YOU DO NOT. YOU HAVE A THING IN YOU THAT SHOULD NOT EXIST AND A SENATOR'S SEAL BURNED IN A BRAZIER ON A MOON YOU WILL NEVER SEE AGAIN AND YOU DO NOT UNDERSTAND ANY OF IT. THAT IS NOT YOUR FAULT. IT IS THE ONLY THING ABOUT YOU THAT IS NOT. The rock stopped. Then, harder, faster, the way it had tapped GIVE IT A NAME in the Cradle: TOMORROW IS THIRTY. THE HAUL ENDS. YOU WILL NOT PASS THIS DOOR AGAIN UNLESS YOU FIND A REASON. FIND ONE.

How?

YOU ARE QUICK. YOU HAVE A MOUTH. YOU HAVE A FOREMAN WITH A BROKEN HAND WHO OWES YOU AND A GUARD WHO BRINGS BANDAGES DOWN THE THROAT. YOU HAVE MORE THAN I HAD. FIND ONE.

And then?

AND THEN, the rock said, IF YOU WANT OUT OF HERE, YOU WILL FIRST LEARN TO COUNT.

I can count. You taught me. Centum.

NOT BREATHS.

He waited. The lamp was coming.

MEN, the rock said. YOU WILL LEARN TO COUNT MEN. WHO OWES. WHO FEARS. WHO HAS A HAND ON WHOM AND WHY. YOU WILL LEARN TO COUNT THEM THE WAY YOU COUNT DEBRIS IN A RING, ALL AT ONCE, WITHOUT LOOKING, AND KNOW WHERE THE GAP IS. THAT IS THE FIRST THING. THE REST IS ONLY BLADES.

The lamp reached him. He picked up the buckets.

Behind him, as he went, the rock tapped one more time, so quietly that he almost did not hear it over his own boots, and it was not a lesson and it was not a joke.

It was a name. Not his. A long one, in the old tongue, three words, tapped once, carefully, by a man who had not said it to anyone in twenty years and wanted it said to someone before the door closed.

He carried it down the road with the buckets, and did not tap it back, and did not say it, and put it with the rest, at the top.
