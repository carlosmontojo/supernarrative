# SERTORIUS — The Slow Burn
## Romance map across six books (pending author approval)

Rule: Jonah Vale and Livia Vibia are the romance of the series. It is present in every
book, it has an "almost" in every book, and it does not happen until Book 6. Every book
has a different reason it cannot happen, and at least one other person on one side or
the other to make it hurt. The reader must never be allowed to relax.

Livia is Priscus's younger sister, daughter of Vibius the elder, the senator the seal
was addressed to. She is the same age as Jonah. Everything Jonah does to his four names
lands on her family twice.

---

## THE TWO OF THEM

### Why they work
- She asks questions she does not know the answer to. Nobody in her caste does.
- He answers questions honestly. Nobody in his position does.
- Both of them think the Law of Spoils is barbaric, and both of them are frighteningly
  good at it.
- She wants to know what the Matter is. He is carrying the answer.

### Why it cannot happen (the rolling reasons)

|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bars. Caste. Seven years. She believes her brother released him. | Chapter 6, through the bars, she asks his name and he gives it. Chapter 25, she reads him and does not say. |

## BOOK 1: DAMNATIO (five touches, two of them charged)

1. Chapter 3, the sharpening. Livia, nineteen, Centurion 40 on a cadet's cutting, bored
   beside her brother at the games. Watches a Collar win a sharpening bout in nine
   seconds. Says "He won" before anyone. Priscus echoes her. After, she finds Jonah at
   the yard edge and asks how he did it, because she is a duellist and it did not look
   like anything she has seen. He is rude to her. She laughs. Then Mara arrives with a
   hull ring and Jonah gets engaged in front of her. Livia watches that too. First
   jealousy, unnamed, on her side.
2. Chapter 5, the investiture. Livia present as her brother's household. Watches the
   reading. For half a second the plate on Jonah's arm is the wrong colour. She notices.
   Says nothing. Files it.
3. Chapter 6, the bars. After the interrogation, before the transport. Livia comes to
   the holding cell with water, the way Villefort's Renée pleaded for Dantès. She tells
   him her brother has promised her he will be released. Jonah believes it because she
   believes it. She asks his name. He gives it. She is the only Stripe who has ever
   asked. He is the last honest face she sees before she learns what her brother is.
   Priscus buries him that night and tells her the boy went home.
4. Chapter 13, year one. Jonah thinks of Mara every night and once, resenting it, of the
   girl at the bars, because she was the last person who used his name.
5. Chapter 25, the inspection. Livia, twenty-five, a scholar-novice of the College on
   the biennial inspection team, reading convicts in the Throat. Jonah, shaved, scarred,
   six years older, holding Hush at grade 31 with everything he has. She reads him. The
   lines flicker. She looks at him for a long moment. He does not know if she knows. She
   writes something in her own notebook, not the College's, and walks on. He does not
   sleep for two days. Tacitus feels his heart and does not understand it.
6. Chapter 34, the interlude. Livia refusing the Claudian match her father arranged.
   She has found her own note again: "Throat, 4471, reading unstable." And a Pit
   register her brother should not have in his study: "Vale, Jonah. Died in the Throat,
   389." She asks Priscus why he told her the boy went home. He lies with Pulse-proof
   calm. She reads him anyway. She knows he lied. Not about what.
7. Chapter 39. Jonah lists what Priscus has: a seat, a Form, a father, a sister. Yara:
   "So?" "So nothing." Tacitus notices his pulse and stores it under the name it has
   heard him not say.

## BOOK 2: PURPLE

- Sertorius arrives in the City. Livia is engaged to Drusus Claudius, the Princeps'
  grandson, Tribune 70, the best young fang on the Ludi floor. She did not choose him.
  She has not declined him either, because declining a Claudian is a political act.
- Jonah courts Cassia Marchand, the banker's daughter, because it is the way into the
  bank. Cassia is clever, bored and not fooled, and likes him anyway. Livia sees them
  at three dinners. Says nothing. Reads him at the third and gets nothing, because now
  he has Quiet. That frightens her more than a bad reading would have.
- Livia and Sertorius talk. He is the only man in the City who answers her questions.
  She is the only person who asks him any. She does not know him. She knows something.
- The Ludi. Drusus challenges Sertorius over precedence. Jonah wins and does not kill
  him, which under the Law of Spoils is an insult to Drusus and a gift to Livia. The
  whole arena understands which. Drusus never forgives it.
- Jealousy: hers, Cassia. His, Drusus, and worse, that Drusus is decent.
- The almost: after the bout, in the tunnels under the arena, she asks him why he
  stopped. He says "Because you would have asked me to." She says "I didn't." He says
  "You would have." That is the whole scene.
- Book ends: Marchand falls, Cassia's family ruined by the man who courted her, Livia
  marries no one, and the Frumentarii open a file on the Sertorian Form.

## BOOK 3: AUXILIA

- Sahra of Tessera. Daughter of the leader Corin betrayed. Jonah finds her in a
  Thinstripe's house on the far Limes and takes her under his roof as a ward, the way
  the Count took Haydée. She is twenty, furious, brilliant, and she loves him openly
  within a year, and says so, and he does not lie to her about it.
- The City calls her his mistress. Livia hears it at every dinner.
- Livia breaks the Claudian engagement. Tells her father it is the College. Tells her
  brother nothing. Tells herself it is not about the man in the stolen house.
- The sparring room. Livia offers to teach Sertorius the Vibian Form, for sport, because
  a man who fights a Form nobody knows should learn one everybody does. Twelve sessions.
  In the last he lets her win. She knows. Neither says.
- Jealousy: hers, Sahra, and Sahra's honesty, which she cannot match. His, Drusus
  again, who has started to court Livia properly, without her father, and is good at it.
- Corin falls on a duelling floor in front of Mara, and Mara asks Sertorius what he is
  now, and Livia is in the stands and sees Mara's face and understands that this man has
  a past that has a woman in it.
- Sahra, at the end: "You will never look at me the way you look at the floor when she
  walks into a room." He does not deny it.

## BOOK 4: PRAETOR

- Priscus at 39, praetor. Jonah's machine: the poisonings in the Vibian house, the
  father's Gracchan seal, the court. Livia is inside the house he is taking apart.
- The night before the end, she comes to his house. She has worked out enough to be
  afraid and not enough to be certain. She asks him the one question. He decides to
  answer it. Tacitus speaks, out loud, for the first time in front of another person:
  "Don't." That is the kiss that does not happen.
- Marcus the younger, Priscus's son, eight, Livia's nephew, dies in the machinery. Not
  by Jonah's hand. By his design.
- Livia finds the seal's ashes, the register, and her own note, and the boy at the bars,
  and Sertorius, and puts them together in one night. Fang at his throat in his own
  house. She does not kill. She has never killed. She leaves.
- Priscus falls. Vibius the elder learns his son burned the seal, from Livia.
- Integrity breaks. Jonah cannot hold plate for a month. Tacitus disagrees out loud.
- Jealousy: none. Nothing is left to be jealous of. That is the book's temperature.

## BOOK 5: LIMES

- War with the Confederacy. Livia is the College's envoy to the Confederate side,
  because she is the only Flamen who has read a harness and the only one they trust.
  Jonah is the man who can unfuse an army and has not decided whose.
- The Confederate envoy, Aster of Sable, is Livia's age, Livia's mind, and has never
  worn a stripe. He courts her honestly. Jonah watches it from across a table for a
  month and says nothing, which Tacitus points out is a thing he does.
- Sahra dies. In the war, on the Limes, holding a door. Jonah carries her name.
- A Confederate ship, a fortnight of forced proximity, one conversation a night. The
  last night: it almost is. The Princeps' offer arrives by courier. She reads the seal
  before he does. She knows what he is being offered. She leaves the cabin.
- Ends with a choice, and her on the other side of it.

## BOOK 6: DEVOTIO

- Whatever is left. The Vein, the names, Tacitus's choice.
- It happens. Late. Not as a reward. As the one thing he did not take.
- Drusus is there for the end and is decent about it, which is the worst thing he
  could do.
- Last page is not theirs. But the second to last is.

## RULES FOR WRITING IT

- No declarations before Book 6. They talk about Forms, the Matter, her brother, his
  ship, the law, the war. Never about themselves. The reader does the work.
- Physical contact is a fang correction, a hand on a shoulder in a fight, once a hand
  on a face in Book 5. That is the budget.
- Every book has one scene where a third party says the obvious out loud (Yara, Sahra,
  Cassia, Tacitus, Drusus). Jonah deflects. Livia does not deny.
- Tacitus is the barometer. It feels his pulse. It learns her name before it learns
  the word "love". In Book 4 it uses the name to stop him.
- In every book she reads him, and the reading gets worse as he gets better at hiding,
  and she trusts the reading less and him more, which is the whole romance in one line.
