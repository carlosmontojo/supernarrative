# SERTORIUS — World Bible
## Lore and worldbuilding (pending author approval)

Rule for using this file: nothing here is exposition. Every entry has a hook, the place
where it touches the plot or a character. Lore enters the text when a character needs
it, argues about it, or gets it wrong. Jonah gets most of it wrong on purpose.

## 2. THE CROSSING AND THE SIX

Six arks. The Empire teaches that only Roma mattered. The Limes teaches the other five.

| Ark | Settled | Descendants today | What they kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roma | Latium, the core | Broadstripes, Thinstripes, most Blanks | The library. Latin. The habit of naming things after a dead empire and then becoming it. |
| Ferrum | Anvil and the Quirinus moons | Anvil Collars | Metalwork, shipbreaking, a stubbornness they are proud of, a habit of naming children after tools and weather. Mara's mother is called Wren, her grandmother Fell. |
| Tessera | Tessera | Tessera Collars | Tile-mosaic art on every wall, a tradition of oath-keeping so strict that Corin's betrayal is a religious crime to them. |
| Marl | Marl | Marl Collars | Farming. The Empire's bread. A song for every field. |
| Halyard | The far Belt | Half Collars, half Confederacy | Sailors. The only people who still build ships without Imperial licence. Yara is Halyard by blood. |
| Sable | Beyond the Limes | The Confederacy | Machines instead of symbionts. They call loricae "the parasite". They are not wrong. Book 5. |

Hook: when the Censor teaches Jonah Latin, Jonah asks why. "Because the men you are
going to lie to think in it. You cannot lie to a man in a language you only speak."

## 3. RELIGION: THE CULT OF THE GENIUS

- Every bearer's genius is a household god. Roman families had lares; the Empire has
  loricae. A Broadstripe house's ancestral genius has a name, a shrine, and a feast day.
  The Vibian genius is called Aequitas. Livia Vibia talked to it as a child, and does not get to wear it: she is the second child, and second children get cuttings.
- A bearer who dies has the lorica cut from him at the pyre and passed to the heir. "The
  god stays, the man goes." The heir wears it within the hour or the genius sulks.
- A Collar who dies has the collar cut off and melted. There is no god to pass on. The
  Limes says a Collar dies twice: once in the body and once in the melt. That is why the
  book of names exists, and why it is the most religious object in the series.
- The Flamens are the priesthood. They alone may cut, invest, fuse, unstrip and read.
  Their college sits on Metalla VII and in the City. Their oath: "The Matter is holy. We
  are its knives." Their real function: keep the Vein cut, keep the fuses in, keep the
  secret.
- The founding myth: on the Crossing the gods spoke to Sabra Claudia and gave her a
  skin of glass. The Flamens know it is a lie and teach it anyway. Some believe it
  anyway. Livia stops believing it at nineteen, in a reading room, and it costs her.
- Sharpening bouts open with a prayer to Mars. Collars are not permitted to say it.
  Jonah says it in chapter 3 in perfect Latin he learned from ship manifests, and the
  crowd does not know whether to laugh.

## 4. GOVERNMENT

- The Princeps: first citizen, holds the Wall, commands the legions, appoints the
  Frumentarii. Tiberius Claudius Septimus, 71, Imperator 131. Has not duelled in forty
  years. Has two sons who hate each other and a granddaughter he prefers.
- The Senate: four hundred houses with the broad stripe. Precedence by duel record.
  Debates are real. Votes are real. The Princeps can veto and rarely needs to.
- The cursus honorum, the ladder of offices, with the minimum ages Rome used: quaestor
  at 30, aedile at 36, praetor at 39, consul at 42. Each office carries imperium, which
  means command gifts. Priscus is quaestor at 32 in chapter 3 and praetor at 39 in
  Book 4. The numbers are the calendar of the series.
- Provinces of the Limes are governed by a legate with a Thinstripe staff. Anvil's
  governor is never seen; the quaestor is the state.
- Publicans: tax farmers. They buy the right to collect a province's taxes and keep
  what they can squeeze. Marchand becomes one. It is the most hated office in the
  Empire and the most profitable.
- The Gracchans, "Republicans": senators who want the Senate to hold the Wall. They
  recruit Collars because Collars are cheap and angry. They would not free one. Vibius
  the elder is their treasurer. His son buried Jonah to protect him.
- The Frumentarii: the grain-men, the Princeps' police. Named for the officers who once
  counted grain shipments and learned everything about everyone. Their charge that
  matters: unregistered genii. They have found three in a hundred years. All three were
  cut. The file on the fourth opens in chapter 40.

## 5. ECONOMY: MATTER IS MONEY

- The unit is the shard: a thumb of raw matter. A shard buys a ship. A cask holds four
  hundred shards. The Pit ships forty casks a quarter. That is the Empire's mint.
- Matter is spent on cuttings (new Blank and Thinstripe loricae), on repairs to
  ancestral loricae (a Broadstripe's plate that sheds in a duel is regrown from raw
  matter), and on the war. A Tribune in the field eats a cask a year.
- Collars are paid in scrip that is only good in company stores. Anvil's company is the
  Morrow yard and three Imperial contractors. Morrow pays above scrip. That is why he
  is loved and why Marchand can ruin him.
- The Vein regrows about what is cut. Nobody knows what happens if the cut outpaces the
  growth. The Censor did the arithmetic in 366. The war in 393 breaks it.
- Anvil's trade: shipbreaking. Imperial hulls come to die in the Quirinus yards. Collars
  cut them apart in vacuum with steel and sweat. What is inside old hulls is Anvil's
  black market, and how the Kestrel's captain knew there were things in the rings worth
  finding.

## 6. GEOGRAPHY

### The Interior
- Latium. The core world. The City sits on its equator, seven hills of towers around
  the Forum, the Senate house, the Flamens' Great College and the Ludi Maximi, an arena
  that seats a million. Book 2 lives here.
- Campania. Bread. Villas. Where Broadstripes are born and where they retire to die.
- Etruria. Shipyards, foundries, the legions' depots. Thinstripe country.

### The Limes
- Anvil (Quirinus IV). Shipbreaking moon. Yards on the surface in vacuum, tenements
  cut into the rock beneath. The rings of Quirinus above, full of four centuries of
  wreckage. Founding Day on the annexation date. Population: eighty thousand Collars,
  four thousand Blanks, two hundred Stripes.
- Tessera. Ring-yards and mosaics. Rises in 390.
- Marl. Farms. The Empire's bread. Quiet, until it is not.
- The Belt. Halyard country. Smugglers, skimmers, the Heron's home.
- Metalla VII. The Pit. A planetoid with the Vein in it. See section 9.

### Beyond
- The Confederacy. Two systems out. Harness technology: mechanical suits, no symbiont.
  Slower than a lorica, cheaper than a lorica, and they can build ten thousand. The war
  of 393 is the Empire discovering that a Tribune cannot be everywhere.

## 7. DAILY LIFE

### On Anvil
- Food: vat protein, ring-ice water, a fungus called yard-bread that grows on hull
  scrap. Real fruit comes from Marl once a quarter and Collars get it if the Blanks are
  full.
- Work: six-day shifts in the yards, the seventh in the tenements. Cutting torches,
  steel, vacuum. A Collar cutter loses a finger a year on average and Jonah has all ten
  because he flies instead.
- Names: Ferrum children are named after tools, weather and ships. Tom, Mara, Corin,
  Wren, Fell, Gaspar, Ochoa are all Anvil names. Latin names on a Collar are a joke or
  an insult.
- Courtship: a hull ring, cut from the first ship you broke, given at engagement. Jonah
  gives Mara one in chapter 3. She is wearing it in chapter 36, on a chain, under her
  husband's roof.
- Death: the melt. Names spoken once at the pyre and then not again, because saying a
  dead Collar's name is said to call the Frumentarii. Superstition, and true.

### In the Interior
- A Broadstripe child is invested at twenty with the house lorica, if the heir; with a
  cutting if not. Second children are called "cuttings" behind their backs.
- Dinner is the duel by other means. Precedence at table by grade. A guest of unknown
  grade is seated last and read by the host's Pulse before the second course. Sertorius
  will be read at every dinner in Book 2.
- Women hold stripes, loricae, seats and fangs. The Law of Spoils has no sex. Livia
  Vibia is a better fang than her brother, on a cutting, and everyone in the house
  knows it and nobody says it.

## 8. LANGUAGE

- Latin is law, liturgy and the Senate. Common (the language of the page) is speech.
  Stripes drop into Latin to exclude. Collars have a dozen loanwords, all insults.
- Register is caste. A Broadstripe speaks in subordinate clauses and never says "I
  think". A Thinstripe speaks like an officer. A Blank speaks like a clerk. A Collar
  speaks plain and short. The Censor's course in Tongue is teaching Jonah to be four
  people.
- "Peregrine" is the legal word for Collar. Saying it to a Collar's face is polite
  contempt. Saying "Collar" is honest contempt. Jonah prefers honest.
- "Stripe" as a verb: to strip. "He got striped" means unstripped. Anvil humour.

## 9. THE PIT: METALLA VII

- A planetoid four hundred kilometres across. The Vein runs through it like a root
  system. The Flamens' college and Tullus's garrison sit on the surface. The mine goes
  down.
- The Throat: the intake shaft, ten kilometres, where the unstripping is done.
- The Shallows: the upper galleries, where the Vein is thin and dead and new convicts
  cut it with steel. Quota is measured in shards.
- The Deep: where the Vein is thick and alive. Deep-diggers work it. It cuts easier and
  it reaches for you. Two years of life, average.
- The Chamber: a sealed cell off the Deep where the Censor lives. Officially it does
  not exist. Tullus visits monthly for the numbers that keep the galleries from
  collapsing.
- The Mouth: the deepest point anyone has reached. The Vein is a wall that breathes.
  Nobody works the Mouth. Chapter 23.
- The harvest: quarterly. Casks sealed on the surface, loaded onto contractor
  freighters. Skimming from casks in transit is a Belt industry.
- The garrison: forty Blank guards, a dozen Thinstripes, Sabinus, Tullus. Guards hold
  Lorica Sight and count the decuries at dawn. Flamens inspect every second year.
- Decuries: ten men, one foreman, one quota. A decury that misses is decimated in the
  old sense: one in ten flogged. A decury that riots is decimated in the older sense.
- Auger's law runs below Tullus's. Rations by challenge. Chisels. The king of the
  condemned is whoever has held the Shallows longest.

## 10. THE LUDI (for Book 2, planted in Book 1)

- The Ludi Maximi in the City, four times a year. Any bearer may enter at the lowest
  step. Steps are bands. Win three at your step and you may challenge the step above.
  The higher wagers his stripe; the lower his life.
- Nobody has climbed from Tiro to Tribune in living memory. Broadstripes enter at
  Centurion for sport. Thinstripes enter for the licence that a Centurion win carries.
- Chapter 3's Lesser Ludi on Anvil are a parody of this with steel. Corin wins them.
  Chapter 3's sharpening is a parody of a parody. Jonah wins that.
- The Forms have followings, banners, bookmakers. A man fighting a Form nobody has seen
  in a century will be noticed by the wrong people within one bout.
