# Chapter 40: Counted by Head

The Heron came off Anvil at the second bell, and Jonah stood at the rail of her hold with the lid of cask thirty-one open beside him and watched the rock go.

It went slowly, then all at once. The lower docks first, the ramp he had gone down in a good coat and up in a hood. The gate with twelve boys reading arms. The tenement stairs, a door left open at the top of one. Founding Square. The melt-house chimney, going as it had for ninety years. Then all of it, the hull ring catching a sun he had seen for three days. Nothing around it had changed. Everything in it had.

The Heron rolled hard through the field wake, and the open cask lurched. Jonah caught the lid with his forearm before it slammed on his hand, and held it there, glass cold against his skin, until the ship settled.

He felt the rock go. That was new. He had flown out of Anvil forty times and never once felt it through his boots. He felt the rock's thousands of hearts fade through the hull now, until one was left. Slow. His.

"You're counted," Pike said, behind him.

Jonah turned.

The old Halyard had a lamp and no paper; he never had paper. "Ten," he said. "Yara counts heads when we lift. Not names; the Empire's got enough of those. She says the number, and if it's the number, we go. It was ten." He looked at the cask. "Nine's the crew. You're the tenth. Counted by head."

"Counted by head."

"That's what a Halyard has." Pike set the lamp on a cask. "It's not much."

Jonah stood at the rail, hand on the glass, and for a moment could not put the thought anywhere. Then he found the place, and put it with the rest.

---

He would learn, a year later, from a small man with a grain-sack and a clerk's smile, exactly how long it had taken.

Four days.

The Heron had been four days off Anvil, lying to a College dock about her casks, when a defrocked Flamen with three shards on a cracked slab took the pen out of his robe for the last time and used it. He wrote it careful, as a Flamen finishes: unlicensed, Anvil, lower docks; bearer unknown; lorica present; genius unregistered; grade indeterminate, lines will not hold still. Then he wrote do not repeat this reading, and under it, in the same hand, repeated it anyway. A hood. A Halyard's grey. A struck citizen's line on the right arm. A Belt turn, learned, not born. Two bodyguards left alive, three shards paid. A Halyard skimmer whose captain ran Hush turned in, the only other arm in eleven years that had read that way.

He sold it for his robe, not a senator's seat; the grain-men did not give those to defrocked Flamens. For his name off the list, and a house in the City he never lived in. It reached a desk nineteen days after a quaestor wrote to his own father in the subjunctive. The small man read it twice and made a fourth page.

Jonah would learn that a year on, from the man himself, over the fish. He would have a broad stripe drawn on his own arm by then, and a woman with a grey book would sit across the table, the one who had said, once, unstable, and had been right. He would think: four days. The old man said a small, frightened, greedy man who has seen you is what ends you. I did the sum and got it wrong on purpose. It took four days.

And he would think: I'd do it again. That's what I'll not tell the old man, on the road, at night, when I say his name. That's the line I keep.

That was a year off, and he did not know it yet. He stood in the Heron's hold with the lid of thirty-one open, and said the names.

---

He said them from the top, the way Decimus had read the Deep, in the voice for things that were not personal, except that every one of them was.

"Tacitus," he said.

It listened.

"Aulus Terentius Pharus. Censor. Master of the Flamens. Read every page, drew the Pit, could not draw a way out of it. Taught a Collar on a rock with a stick for five years, and beat him. Laughed once, coughed blood, said the name of a house so it had been said. Wanted to save the Empire. Got me instead, and knew it. It's on the slab in my hand."

"Tom Vale. Anvil. Morrow's yard. Nine fingers. South yards. Believed in the ladder. Sold a set of numbers for a winter's food and knew, and never told me. Was happy the night I answered in Latin. Sat in a chair with the door open. Paid by the yard, the melt says. Paid by the man who wrote the letter, out of his own purse, because a good man made him. Mara said his name at the fire where you don't."

"Captain Ochoa. The Kestrel. Said nothing, the highest thing. Carried a seal for a senator, died on a decurion's fang over the fish course. Said Vibius with the last of it. Morrow said his name right, once, at the melt, and didn't look at me."

"Decurion Varro. The Vigilant. Killed a man without breaking conversation. The first. His feet are in my legs and his contempt is not, anymore, and that took the old man a year."

He said the road. Corr, north yards, four years, said a month, a tree. Bricks. Hollen. Aulus of Marl. Tallow, fifteen. Marcus, who never used it. The Deep man written as unknown. Auger, who said his name to Ulli and made a Centurion hurry with a second chisel. Vetch, a guard, not written before, and was now. Sorrel. The boy from the last transport whose name he did not know, and said so.

"Ullan of Marl. The east fields. Two l's; they always get it wrong. Nine years in a two-year Deep because he sang, and the wall listened and never finished. Carried forty-three. Forty-four. Chose the day. Sat down with his back to it on the eighteenth and stopped singing on the fourth verse of the fallow song, Pell on one side and me on the other. It was his. There was something he did on Marl, and it was his, and he kept it. He asked me, once, to take one. I took it."

"Decimus Alba. Of Etruria. Sula's father." He said that first, as he'd been told to. "Then the rest. Guard on the Kestrel. Guard on the Throat. Said my number seven years and said please, the only one who did. It was never personal, and I never once believed him. Signed for five more so a tree wouldn't die. Wrote me into the wall in the morning, so the road would say so, whichever way the night went. Came down slow, rifle down, to say the number first, and got as far as the breath. It's not on him. It's on me. He said write it right. This is right."

He said Gnaeus Sabinus, client of the Princeps' house, who said oh. The two boys at the road's end who did not run, their bands a week old. Pollio, who frowned and wrote it down and was right. Rennick, who wrote them light. Harrow, in, with less, and a girl on Marl named Ada, her message on a Halyard ship going the wrong way with a sister who owes Pike. Pell, north forty, never made quota, who sang the last verse to the wall with his chin up so it would know he was there. Tullus, who counted casks, not the men who packed them, and was a decent man; he'd be a prefect with a letter beside his name. He did not know yet what he would do about Tullus, and said so.

He said Kaspar, half a cutting, one word of the old tongue. Dace, and the boy who said what.

"Bram. A liar, everybody says. Knew me from six, looked into a hood from a hand's width, shut his mouth and said, bloody Halyards. Carried Corin home once. Said my father's name in the road to two boys with fangs."

"Ezra Morrow. Blank. Paid Collars like citizens and was poor. Made a purser pay for a fire to his face in a clean office, put it on his ledger as a gift to the yard, the only line he's not ashamed of. Told Mara I was alive. Has a daughter with a face that says what something is, out loud, in a room. Has a yard, for a while longer."

He said Gaspar Roake: what he did, which was nothing; what he said, which was you've got his hands; what he had, a bottle, and not much.

He said Corin Aldane: the stripe, the quota, the word friend. A boy of three, and the face from the first sand.

He said Silas Marchand: purser, precise, a bank.

He said Marcus Vibius Priscus, with what he had done, plainly and in order, as the old man said to name a man you're going to lie to. At the end of it he stopped, and did not say the other thing. Tacitus put the heart where it kept it, under the name it had heard him not say, and did not say it either.

He said Mara Ansel. He said the Halcyon.

He said the six hundred and forty-two from the slabs, every one, as he had said them at the bottom of a well without a mouth.

Then he said his own.

"Jonah Vale. Anvil. Morrow's yard. Purser's pilot. Citizen for a week. Four four seven one. Went into the wall on the nineteenth night of the harvest, in a guard's hand. Died in the Throat five years before that, in a quaestor's. That's in the book, a name like the rest of them, and it stays, because names stay." He put his hand flat on the open cask. "And the one who carries it is called something else now. Not in the book; not a name, a stripe. The old man said a stripe is something you wear, and a name is something you are. I've got both, and I know which is which."

He stopped.

"That's the count," he said. "It starts with you and it ends with me and there's room. There's always room. It's the only thing about a count that's kind."

---

Tacitus did not do the small thing.

He waited for it, the way a man waits for a hand on his arm. It did not come. He felt, instead, the deciding. It stood in the doorway of him as it had on a slab at the Throat when a word came, on a road when a fang came, at the bottom of a well when a guard went through it. It decided, and he felt it decide, and did not know what.

Then it spoke.

Not one word. He would keep that, the same as he kept the water step done right, and a laugh on a rock that went on till it coughed. It was the second time it had spoken in four hundred years, and it did not say the word it had. It said something it had heard, in the old tongue, in the voice that was very old and had been used once and knew it. What it said was something it had heard him say, on a rock and on a road, more times than any other, said to it when it did the hand with no hand and had no hand.

It said: I know.

Jonah stood very still.

Then he laughed. Once, out loud, at the beam, as he had laughed on the Cradle floor when it had learned to hold him from its mother and stopped when he said when. It had learned to speak from him. The old man had said it would: it has no tongue but yours. The first sentence it made was the one he had given it, every time, for something that could not be answered. It had given it back.

"Yes," he said. "All right. Yes. You do."

Pike, at the foot of the ladder, said, to the lamp: "He's talking to it."

"He always has," Yara said, from the top of the ladder, where she had been standing as long as it took a man to say six hundred and ninety names. "Pike. Come up. Leave him the lamp."

Pike came up.

Yara stood a moment longer, hand on the rail, looking down at a man with a stripe nobody could see yet, beside an open cask, his hand on the Vein. She did not say the obvious; she had said it once, and a Halyard doesn't repeat itself.

"Thirty days to the College dock, Sertorius," she said. "Learn your plate. Sleep in the cask. And say them somewhere I can hear, next time. I'd like to know what they sound like from the top."

She went up. The hatch shut.

Jonah stood in the hold with his hand on the glass and the lamp on the beam. The Heron went out into the Belt toward the Interior, at the pace a Halyard ship goes when it is going somewhere. He felt the hull through his palm. The nine hearts above him. His own, one. And Tacitus, named now, who had just said two words to him. He thought of a woman with a grey book who had said unstable. A small man with a file that would soon have four pages. A City he had never seen, four hundred houses in a chamber with a wall behind them that said come to every cutting in the Empire and heard nothing. A niche in the ninth row, third from the eastern wall, where something had slept three hundred and ninety years that no glass could read.

"I'm not ready," he said. To the dark. In the old tongue. So it was said.

Tacitus, which had one word of its own and one sentence of his, had just found out which it preferred. It said it again.

I know.

---

He would learn the rest of it over the same fish, a year on, and keep it with the four days. Nineteen days after the Heron lifted off Anvil, a courier packet crossed a desk in the City with the province's post. The small man read the paper twice and wrote the fourth page, under do not repeat this reading:

Repeated. A Halyard skimmer, the Heron. Captain runs dark. Bound for the College dock at Quirinus Interior with thirty-eight casks and a story. Tenth head not on the slate.

And under that, because he was a man who decided what was true before he asked, and had never once had anything go into a wall that he would have liked to see go into one:

He did not go into the wall.

Jonah would hear that from the man's own mouth, with the woman across the table. He would say it in the old tongue, with the endings right, what he had said to a Halyard bosun in a hold with a bowl of fish going cold in his hands. He would go on saying it, on every road, at night, until the book was full and he was the last page:

That's the count. It's not much. It's what a man has.
