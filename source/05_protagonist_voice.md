# Jonah's Voice: the mouth, the ladder, and the humility

The single rule: **the bite goes up, never down, and it costs him.**

Jonah talks. That is the character. He is not brave; he is unable to let a thing
stand unsaid, and it has cost him his citizenship, his father, and seven years.
The humour is not decoration. It is what his fear does with his mouth.

---

## 1. THE FOUR MOVES

Everything he says is one of these. Vary them; never let two of the same kind run
together.

1. **Answer a question with a question.** He does not accept the other man's frame.
   "Habrás oído hablar del Pulso." / "¿Quién se lo ha dicho?"
2. **Name the power in the room, politely.** The courtesy is the insult.
   "Los Collares no oímos hablar de muchas cosas. Es parte del diseño."
3. **Volunteer a true thing nobody asked for.** He takes control by over-sharing.
   "Estoy nervioso. Me pongo hablador. Es lo peor que hago y es lo único que traigo."
4. **The flat joke, acknowledged.** He makes it, it lands badly, and he says so.
   "¿Eso ha sido una broma?" / "Ha sido un intento."

## 2. THE LADDER: bite scales with grade

| Part | Grade | Power | How the mouth behaves |
|---|---|---|---|
| I, ch. 1–6 | Tiro 0–1 | none | Runs constantly. Cannot stop it. It gets him collared, arrested and buried. He is not defiant on purpose; he simply answers back, and everyone who loves him has told him it will kill him. |
| II, ch. 7–20 | 1–28 | hidden | The Pit takes it away. The reader **sees the line he does not say**, then sees him say the safe one. When he does speak, it is to put himself between a guard and somebody smaller. |
| III, ch. 21–28 | 28–32 | secret | The Censor gives it back as a weapon: Bearing, rhetoric, the Count. He needles Tullus, Rennick, Harrow with a purpose, and it works, and it frightens him that it works. |
| III–IV, ch. 29–34 | 41–42 | real | He has thirty rifles in front of him and says the count aloud that the prefect will not say. To Pollio, to Sabinus, to Yara: full bite, no hedging. |
| IV, ch. 35–40 | 42 | total on this rock | The most dangerous man on Anvil pays a drunk for a story and gives a ruined yard a slab. Bite at its highest; humility at its most visible. |

**The dial:** in Part I he is mouthy and it costs him. In Part IV he is lethal and
he is still the man who carries the buckets. If a reader ever thinks "what a
smug bastard", the pass has failed.

## 3. THE HUMILITY RULES (non-negotiable)

These are what keep him from being a cocky bastard. Every one is already in the
plot; the pass must make them audible.

- **Never punches down.** He does not mock Collars, guards, boys with week-old
  bands, drunks, or servants. The joke goes at the man with the fang and the
  stripe, or at himself. When a Tiro boy fails to frighten him, he does not
  humiliate him: "Es un chaval con una banda. Es lo que yo habría sido."
- **He does the menial work and never mentions it.** The buckets, the haul, the
  bowl given back.
- **He admits fear out loud.** "Estoy nervioso." "No estoy preparado." This is the
  opposite of swagger and it is why the swagger is bearable.
- **He asks people their names, and uses them.** Ullan with two l's. Ada. Sula.
- **He says thank you and please to people with no power**, and does not to
  people with a great deal.
- **After every cruelty he counts the cost aloud.** The book of names is the
  humility engine of the novel. He never wins a fight and gloats; he wins and
  then says who is dead.
- **He apologises fast and plainly when he is wrong.** No speeches.
- **He is wrong sometimes and the text lets him be.** Nasso: he does the sum and
  gets it wrong on purpose, and it takes four days to ruin him.

## 4. WHAT HE IS NOT

- Not a clown. He does not do bits, he does not riff, he is never the funniest
  man in the scene on purpose.
- Not sarcastic at the expense of the weak.
- Not unflappable. The mouth runs **because** he is frightened. When he is calm
  he is quiet, and that is when other people get nervous.
- Not a monologuer. Two or three sentences, then let someone answer.
- Never smug after a win.

## 5. SAMPLE LINES, BY PART

**Part I (nothing to lose but everything to lose)**

- To Varro, a decurion with a fang: "Nadie toca nada que usted no diga que se
  toque." (pause) "Eso incluye al purser. Sobre todo al purser."
- To the inspector: "Seis años y diez dedos, señor. Los cuento cada mañana."
- To Priscus, being told his own preferences: "¿Quién se lo dice? Perdón. Es que
  usted es la primera persona que me habla en dos días y ya sabe lo que me gusta."
- After the sharpening bout, to Livia: "Intentaré ser más feo la próxima vez."

**Part II (the line he does not say)**

- Bricks: "Cut a shard, then. If you can." What Jonah does not say: *Puedo cortar
  tu cuota y la mía y aún me sobra la tarde.* What he says: "Sí."
- To a guard who shoves Pell: "Foreman of six, sir. If he goes down, the count
  goes down." (Bite hidden inside procedure. This is how he talks in the Pit.)
- To Ulli, who has just fed him: "Te has comido mi cuenco." / "Te lo ibas a
  dejar." / "Me lo iba a dejar, sí. Que conste que era mío."

**Part III (the mouth comes back)**

- To Rennick, deputy prefect: "Escriba lo que van a pesar. Nadie ha comprobado
  nunca una pizarra contra una barrica en esta roca, porque a nadie le ha
  importado nunca lo que pesa una barrica. Hasta este año."
- To Tullus, with thirty rifles: "Yo diría los números en voz alta, prefecto,
  porque usted no va a decirlos. Pell primero. Es viejo y canta."
- To Sabinus: "Un hombre que usted no conoció, Gneo Sabino. Que leyó todas las
  páginas. Y encontró una sobre usted."

**Part IV (power, and the same man)**

- To Pollio, a Flamen with a rod: "Muriéndome. Seis años. Lo dice todo el mundo."
- To Nasso, who has a bell under the table: "Sabe lo que vale. Lo sabía antes de
  ponerme la mano en el brazo. Y no ha tocado la campana. Eso es lo que estoy
  comprando."
- To Yara: "Le he dicho la verdad de lo que puedo hacer. Le habría mentido si
  colara."
- To Gaspar, who does not know who he is: "Tienes las manos de su padre." /
  "Los Halyard tienen manos. Son los barcos."

## 6. THE RUNNING JOKE

He counts. Everybody on the road knows he counts. The joke the other characters
make about him, all book, is that he is counting them right now. He always is.

- Pell: "Tienes la cara."  / "¿Qué cara?" / "La de sumar."
- Pike, three times: "He talks."
- Yara: "He's stopped talking. Write that down. Es la primera vez."

## 7. BUDGET

- At least **three** mouth-beats per chapter in Parts I, III and IV.
- In Part II, at least **two** unsaid lines per chapter, plus one place where he
  speaks up for somebody smaller.
- At most **one** joke per scene. Never two in a row.
- Every chapter with a kill in it needs a humility beat in the same chapter.
